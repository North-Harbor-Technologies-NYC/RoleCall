# Positioning

This file is yours. The rubric, the resume skill, and the outreach skill read it on every run. Keep the headings; replace the content. The example rows describe a content-operations executive; swap in your own.

## Target archetypes

Three to five. Each archetype is a kind of role you would take, named the way the market names it. The rubric scores archetype fit (primary 5, secondary 3.5, adjacent 2.5) and uses the "lead with" column to decide which proof points open the resume PROFILE paragraph.

| Archetype | Fit | Key signals in the JD | What they are buying |
|---|---|---|---|
| VP / Head of Content Operations | PRIMARY | "content operations", "production infrastructure", "workflow", "supply chain" | Someone who builds and scales the systems that make delivery work |
| Head of Post-Production | PRIMARY | "post-production", "editorial", "Head of Post", "broadcast" | A proven operator who can run a large post org and deliver |
| Enterprise Account Executive, media | SECONDARY | "enterprise", "account executive", "strategic accounts" | A credible insider who has lived on the buyer side |
| Solutions Consultant | ADJACENT | "enablement", "advisory", "transformation", "change management" | Someone who can teach and lead adoption inside a complex org |

## Lead with, by archetype

| If the role is... | Lead with... |
|---|---|
| VP / Head of Content Operations | The system you built, the scale it reached, the number that proves it |
| Head of Post-Production | Years of operational depth, the slate, the awards, the relationships |
| Enterprise AE | Buyer-side credibility, the executive relationships, the deals you closed |
| Solutions Consultant | Translator between what the tools do and what the work needs; who you trained |

## Exit narrative

Two or three sentences, in your voice, used in every PROFILE paragraph and cover letter. Say what you ran, what changed because of you, and what you want next. No adjectives that a stranger could not verify.

## Green flags

- A posted salary range at or above your target.
- The role reports to someone who owns the outcome you deliver.
- The JD names a problem you have solved, in its own words.

## Red flags

- Four or more days on site outside your home metro.
- "Player-coach" at VP level with no reports.
- Requirements the master resume does not show and cannot be bridged (list them).
- A posting older than 60 days with no repost.

## Location rules

Used by the rubric's location dimension (1 to 5) and mirrored in `rubric/blockers.yml`.

| Situation | Score |
|---|---|
| Home metro, hybrid 1 to 3 days | 5 |
| US remote | 4.5 |
| Home metro, 4+ days on site | 4 |
| Remote with regular travel | 4 |
| Another US metro, flexible | 2.5 |
| Another US metro, on site | blocked |
| Outside the US | blocked |

## Red-flag questions

The questions an interviewer will ask that need a prepared answer. The evaluate skill writes the answers into Block F of every report and the story bank keeps the spoken versions.

- Why did you leave your last role?
- What have you been doing since?
- Do you have direct reports today?

## Negotiation

- Never give a number first; ask for the range.
- Anchor on the top of the posted range plus the bonus structure.
- If downleveled: accept only at or above the floor, with a written six-month review and promotion criteria.
